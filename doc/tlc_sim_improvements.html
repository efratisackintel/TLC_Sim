Idea 1 — Handle collision Open

Today (firmware). The RS_MNG rate scaler drives its decision from a single Success Ratio (acked ÷ txed) aggregated over its statistics windows. That SR blends channel loss (rate-dependent) with collision loss (rate-independent) — the firmware does not separate the two causes. So when contention pulls the SR down, RS_MNG can read it as "rate too fast" and trigger the same down-scale / search cycle as a genuine channel fade, even though a lower MCS cannot recover a collision. The idea: detect collision-dominated loss and stop down-scaling for it (protect with RTS/CTS instead).

The problem in one picture

During the burst SR = (1−PER)·(1−0.40) = 0.6 at every MCS. Since 0.6 < 90%, the current logic keeps dropping MCS — but SR never recovers (loss is collisions), so it bottoms out at MCS 1. The proposed logic recognises the loss is rate-independent, holds the top rate, and just rides the collision hit — tracking best.

Proposed algorithm change
  1. Split the signal. Keep the throughput on the blended SR, but drive the rate decision on the channel part only: SR_rate = 1 − PER, estimated from the loss that is correlated across rates.
  2. Detect collision-dominated loss. If failures are (a) roughly equal at high and low MCS, (b) all-or-nothing per A-MPDU (BA bitmap), and (c) the medium was busy/again busy around TX (CCA) — classify as contention, not channel.
  3. Don't down-scale for it. On collision-classified loss: hold the current column/MCS (no forced decrease, no search cycle), and…
  4. Protect instead. Turn on RTS/CTS (or raise its threshold) to shrink the collision window; keep the high rate for the data.
  5. Still react to real fades. If loss grows as MCS rises (rate-correlated) or RSSI drops → treat as channel and down-scale as usual.

Control checks (must not regress)
  • Genuine fade: drop SNR (no collision) → B must still down-scale exactly like A (no false "collision" hold).
  • Clean link: SNR high, collision 0 → A and B identical (idea 1 is inert when there's no contention).
  • Mixed: fade and collision together → B separates causes: down-scale for the fade, but no extra collapse from the collision.
