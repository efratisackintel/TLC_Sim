TLC fix proposals — the wide-BW (320 MHz) trap

Case: ZoharBadLog.sysmon (Scorpius2-Whp, 6 GHz, 13.3 s, uplink). Two throughput drops of ~1 s each, caused by committing to 320 MHz instead of 160 MHz. Simulation: TLC playground, mode 3 (real trace) — the same scenario and the same trap moments for every algorithm, mean of 200 Monte-Carlo seeds. All 9 fixes are checkboxes in the playground (Engine options & fixes).

What goes wrong (from the log)

  1. Lucky test. One short 320 MHz test window (20 frames, ~2 ms) passes, so the search cycle commits to 320 MHz.
  2. Wrong dimension. On 320 MHz the success rate falls to ~20%. BA-based stats can't tell "MCS too high" from "upper 160 MHz busy", so the TLC lowers MCS down to MCS 0 and stays on 320 MHz.
  3. Frozen. The SR drop sets avoid-higher-MCS (rateScaleMng.c ~L5593), which blocks every search cycle for 1 s (RS_MNG_UPSCALE_IGNORE_HIGHER_MCS).
  4. Steep retry table. The 320 MHz retry table falls straight from 320 MHz to 20 MHz (9 duplicate entries), so throughput in the trap is only ~10 Mbps.
  5. The warning was already there. The FW sends RTS before every A-MPDU: 81% of RTS on 320 MHz got no CTS (160 MHz: 26%). Even when a CTS came back, 83% of 320 MHz A-MPDUs got no BA (160 MHz: 9%). None of this reaches the rate decision.

FIX 5 and FIX 7 never change a clean or collision-only scenario. FIX 8 gains 19% under collision bursts (a column switch that turns bad is undone in ~80 ms) but loses 6% on the sudden wall: when the whole channel drops, it restores GPR1 — a rate that was good before the drop and now fails too — instead of letting the search find a robust one. The fixes

FIX 1 — Confirm a wider BW before committing simulated

WhatA wider-BW test column must pass 3 test windows in a row before it can be committed. WhyOne 2 ms test is weak evidence when the secondary channel has bursty interference. Who it helpsLinks next to a bursty neighbour network on the secondary channel. RiskSlower to adopt a clean wider BW. In simulation the 3 back-to-back tests (~7 ms) fall into the same quiet gap, so it barely helps. Needs confirmation spread over time (e.g. one per search cycle).

FIX 2 — Escape the MCS-0 floor simulated

WhatAt MCS 0 with SR < 50%, start a search at once even if avoid-higher-MCS is set; try narrower/other columns only. WhyAvoid-higher-MCS stops upward oscillation, but at MCS 0 there is nothing lower to fall to — the 1 s freeze only locks in a failing link. Who it helpsAny column stuck at its floor: wide BW, MIMO at low SNR, after a fade. RiskLow: healthy links never reach this state.

FIX 3 — Wider-BW backoff simulated

WhatA wider BW abandoned < 3 s after it was committed is barred from search for 2 s, doubling up to 16 s. WhySecondary-channel interference is usually a static neighbour network: if 320 MHz just failed, it will fail again. Who it helpsRepeated traps (the second drop in this log). RiskAlways takes the first trap; can wait up to 16 s before retrying a channel that became clean.

FIX 4 — GPR safety net in the retry table simulated

WhatKeep the primary rate + measured throughput of the last 10 search cycles (past statistics). The best two are GPR1/GPR2. Put them in retry-table slots 3–4, only if their expected throughput is ≤ rate0's. WhyToday the trap's retry table jumps 320 → 20 MHz. With GPR1 = M1·160 in slot 3, failed frames are delivered on a rate that is known to work. Who it helpsAny bad switch (wider BW, MIMO, GI): raises throughput while the TLC is on a bad primary. A plain 160 MHz fallback raised in-trap throughput 10 → 24 Mbps. RiskDoes not shorten the trap (time on 320 MHz 1.90 → 1.63 s), only raises throughput in it. History goes stale after a real fade — drop GPRs whose retries also fail. SimulatedTrap table becomes M0·320 → M1·160 → M0·160·MIMO → M0·320·long GI → M0·20. 88 → 91 Mbps; neutral or better elsewhere.

FIX 5 — Ban bad primary rates (BPR) simulated

WhatA primary rate whose measured throughput falls below a threshold of GPR1's (e.g. 50%) is marked BPR and banned from operational use for 2 s (or 4 search cycles). WhyForces the TLC off M0·320 after 1–2 stat windows (~0.1–0.2 s) instead of ~1 s, and stops re-entry. Who it helpsGeneralises FIX 2 + FIX 3 to any rate dimension. RiskIn a real fade every rate looks bad against a stale GPR1: never ban the most robust rate, require ≥ 2 windows, and don't ban if GPR1 is also failing. Ban at the dimension level (BW = 320), not one MCS. SimulatedBan only at the BW / stream-count level, 2 windows, 2 s. Drop 1.92 → 0.81 s, 88 → 94 Mbps — the best of the retry/history fixes; no change on any other scenario.

FIX 6 — Gradual step-down (SD) retry table, continue on the line that succeeded simulated

WhatReplace duplicate retry entries with step-down rates: MCS −1, BW/2, antennas 2→1 (alternate antennas at the lowest rate), GI 0→1→2→3; repeat until a rate not yet in the table. Keep rate0 ×2 and the low/legacy entries. The LMAC starts the next A-MPDU on the line that succeeded, not back on rate0 (same table). WhyOld table: 320 → 9 × M0·20 MHz → legacy, and every A-MPDU starts again on 320. New table: 320 → 160 → 80 → 40 → 20. When 320 fails and 160 succeeds, the good rate is 160 — the following A-MPDUs go out on 160 instead of failing on 320 again. Who it helpsAny sudden degradation of the primary rate: throughput recovers in milliseconds, without waiting for the TLC or the 1 s avoid freeze. RiskCollisions: a burst moves the LMAC down to a much slower line, and it only climbs back after successes (here: one line up after 4). The TLC gets few samples of rate0 while the LMAC stays lower, so its statistics must include the lines actually used. Check the GI code order: code 2 (0.8 µs, 4× LTF) has a shorter GI than code 1 (1.6 µs). SimulatedBack up one line after 4 successes; TLC stats = rate0 first attempts only. 88 → 92 Mbps, drop 1.92 → 1.41 s; with FIX 4 (GPR1 = M1·160 right after 320): 95 Mbps, 0.96 s. Costs ~20% under collisions and ~3% on a clean SNR sweep — consider stepping MCS first and BW only when MCS is already 0, and a faster way back up (e.g. retry rate0 every N A-MPDUs). The simulator has 2 GI values and no per-antenna channel, so the GI and alternate-antenna steps are approximated.

FIX 7 — CTS guard (RTS/CTS statistics per BW) simulated

WhatTrack the share of RTS answered by a CTS per BW (EWMA). If the wider BW's CTS ratio is < 50% of the next narrower BW's: (a) never commit it after a search test; (b) if already on it, leave at once (search narrower/other columns, not blocked by avoid-higher-MCS). WhyBA statistics can't separate "MCS too high" from "secondary channel busy"; CTS can. In this log the receiver didn't answer 81% of RTS on 320 MHz vs 26% on 160 MHz — for 7 s before the first trap. Comparing BWs cancels collisions on the primary channel, which hit both. Who it helpsWide-BW links (160/320 MHz) next to a neighbour network on the secondary channel — the root cause of this case. No effect on clean channels (TVA and synthetic presets identical). RiskUses data the FW already has (RTS is already sent for every A-MPDU here) — no extra airtime. Needs RTS protection to be on; the simulator assumes CTS fails exactly when the wider BW is busy at the receiver (consistent with the log: 81% no-CTS vs fitted 79% loss). Open question for FW: static vs dynamic BW signalling in RTS — the log shows every CTS at 20 MHz, and data after a CTS on 320 MHz is still sent at 320 MHz.

FIX 8 — Early termination of fatal degradation simulated

WhatDetect a drastic fall in the last T = 80 ms (search cycle = 500 ms): current average SR ≤ 30% × GPR1's average SR, or current average tpt ≤ 30% × GPR1's average tpt. On such a fatal error, terminate at once and restore GPR1's TLC operation table — no waiting for the next search cycle or the 1 s avoid-higher-MCS freeze. WhyIn the trap the SR on 320 MHz falls to ~20% while GPR1 (M1·160) had ~95%; the firmware sits there ~1.3 s because every search is blocked. GPR1 is a rate that was measured good a moment ago, so going straight back to it is safe. Who it helpsAny column switch that turns out badly: wider BW into OBSS, MIMO into a correlated channel, a lucky test. Also short collision bursts right after a switch. RiskWhen the whole channel drops (not just the new column), GPR1 is stale and fails too — it restores the wrong rate before the normal search finds a robust one. Guard: only within one search period of a column switch, or require GPR1 to differ only in the dimension that just changed. SimulatedOnly after a search committed a new column (BW / NSS / GI); averages over the windows of the last 80 ms; GPR1 = best rate of a different column in the last 10 search cycles. Trap 7.63–8.95 s becomes 7.63–8.02 s. 88 → 96 Mbps, drop 1.92 → 0.65 s. Side effects: +19% on collision bursts, −6% on the sudden-wall scenario (stale GPR1). The slide says "restore BPR1" — simulated as restoring GPR1 (the good rate); please confirm.

FIX 9 — Collision-aware downscale simulated

WhatAn A-MPDU that got no BA at all (whole transmission lost: collision / BA timeout) is left out of the success rate the TLC uses for MCS up/down decisions — as long as such A-MPDUs are ≤ 50% of the window. If most of the window is lost this way, it is counted as today (sustained loss is a real problem). WhyA BA with holes means "MCS too high"; no BA at all usually means "someone else transmitted". Today one collided A-MPDU in a window drops the SR to ~80%, the MCS is lowered and the higher MCS is put on a 1 s cooldown — at high SNR the link drifts far below what the channel supports. Who it helpsEvery busy channel: +70% on "steady" (5% collisions), +38% on "interference burst", +10% / +217% at 45 dB with 2% / 10% collisions. No change on clean channels, fades or the TVA sweep. RiskHidden-node losses that hit every A-MPDU partially still count (correct). A very high MCS that kills the preamble also gives "no BA" — the 50% rule keeps that case as today. On the 320 MHz trap it is slightly negative alone (drop 1.92 → 2.24 s: fewer early downscales on 320) — combine with FIX 7. The FW has the signal today: BA timeout vs BA bitmap.

Suggested order

  1. FIX 7 (CTS guard) — targets the root cause, biggest gain, no airtime cost, no side effects.
  2. FIX 9 (collision-aware downscale) — the largest gain outside this case (busy channels, high rates); no side effect on clean links.
  3. FIX 8 (early termination) or FIX 5 / FIX 2 — leave a bad rate fast. FIX 8 is the fastest (80 ms) and also helps collision bursts, but needs a guard against restoring a stale GPR1 after a real channel drop; FIX 5 is more general (any BW / stream count); FIX 2 is the smallest change.
  4. FIX 4 — known-good rates in the retry table; small gain, no downside seen.
  5. FIX 6 — together with FIX 4, after reducing the collision cost (faster way back up) and the BW-halving cost on SNR-limited links.
  6. FIX 3 — re-entry protection, if FIX 5 is not taken.
  7. FIX 1 — only in its time-spread form, and only if the lab shows short quiet gaps.
Before shipping: clean-channel regression (TVA sweep), more 320 MHz drop logs, and a lab test with a bursty interferer on the upper 160 MHz.
